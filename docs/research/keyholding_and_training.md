# Keyholding, D/s training and brat taming: research notes (2026-11-15)

Asked by the owner: "do a research on being a keyholder, how to train BDSM slaves, how to tame a brat and anything else". Most sources are
community blogs, podcasts and vendor pages, not research; the psychology section uses textbook material. What follows are the points the
sources agree on, then what they mean for this system.

## What the sources agree on

**Keyholding**
- Agree the "what ifs" before locking: app failure, pain, emergencies. A quick, reliable way out in an emergency is part of good keyholding.
- Check-ins scale with the length of a session: daily for short locks, once or twice a day for week-plus locks; remote keyholders keep
  contact regular and emergency release independent of the keyholder.
- Build duration in steps and extend on feedback.
- Daily skin checks; numbness, colour change, broken skin or pain means unlock now. Fit matters more than anything else.
- A keyholder who ignores distress destroys trust; denial that causes real distress is paused and reassessed.

**Training a submissive**
- Trust and connection come before rules. Protocols are added gradually: first simple ones (reporting, asking permission), more
  nuanced ones as the dynamic matures.
- Reinforcement usually works better than punishment: reward the wanted behaviour, let the unwanted one go unrewarded. Punishment is kept
  for real rule breaks and must be predictable.
- Discipline aims at self-discipline: over time the structure should be needed less, because the habits hold on their own.
- Conditioning without informed consent is abuse; agree goals, and know how training would be undone if it ends.

**Brat taming**
- Consistency is the core. A brat pushes to find the edges; a keyholder who is strict one day and absent the next gives no edges, and the
  game turns into confusion. Every rule break gets the same, agreed response, every time.
- Attention is the brat's reward. Arguing, explaining and negotiating reward the push; a short, calm, certain consequence does not.
- Taming is not only punishment: the response should be satisfying for both. "Funishment" (play the brat enjoys) and real punishment
  (a consequence that corrects) are different tools and should not be mixed up.
- Expectations and limits are agreed beforehand; the consequences are ones both agreed to.

**Psychology of reward (operant conditioning)**
- Rewards on an unpredictable schedule (variable ratio) make a behaviour the most resistant to dying out; fixed rewards are learned faster
  but fade faster when they stop. The "it is like gambling" claim is contested, the extinction resistance is well established.
- Reward soon after the behaviour works better than reward much later.

## How this system already matches

- **Consistency:** consequences are decided by code and closed catalogues, never by mood; a rule break always becomes the same question,
  the same judge with one appeal, the same Warning or incident, the same lines. The model can't negotiate rules.
- **Brat attention:** guardrails forbid arguing and accusing without evidence; the deterministic paths (commands, buttons) answer before any
  model.
- **Safety first:** health and emergency access is immediate; any pain cancels a task without consequence; hygiene openings, relock pictures.
- **Gradual protocols:** spot checks, photo check-ins, daily tasks, the plug ladder, behind-back cuffs only after 3 verified tries,
  conditioning raising the bar slowly.
- **Reinforcement:** tokens, training bonuses, the clean streak, recovery credits, "Your task was verified. Good."

## Gaps these notes suggest (proposals, need the owner's yes)

1. **Unpredictable rewards.** Today every reward is fixed and known. A small random bonus after some verified tasks (for example about 1
   in 5 verified obedience tasks adds +0.05 to the pool, unannounced until it happens) would make obedience more resistant to fading, per
   the reinforcement research.
2. **No debate with a brat line.** When he argues or pushes back on a rule, the keyholder replies once, briefly, restates the rule and
   the consequence, and does not engage further in that turn. Today the model may still explain at length.
3. **A protocol ladder.** Add small daily protocols one at a time as earlier ones hold: the morning report (approved), then for example
   asking permission before a hygiene opening with a reason, a weekly reflection answer in the Sunday review, a fixed form of address.
   Each new protocol only after the last one held for two weeks.
4. **Wellbeing check in the weekly review.** One question on Sunday ("How are you doing with the training this week?"), free answer, never
   scored; a hard answer leads to a calm check, not a consequence. Sources: ignoring distress erodes trust; denial distress is paused.
5. **Check-in frequency by lock length.** Photo check-ins could scale with how long the current lock has run (more on week-plus locks).
6. **Daily skin check reminder.** During each hygiene opening, a one-line reminder to check for redness, numbness or broken skin, and that
   any of it means staying unlocked to heal without consequence.
